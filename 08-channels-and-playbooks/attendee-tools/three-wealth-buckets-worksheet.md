# The Three Wealth Buckets Worksheet

## Purpose

This worksheet helps attendees map where their wealth sits today. The goal is not simply to accumulate more wealth, but to build the right assets for the right timeframe.

## Prompt

> Is your wealth available when you actually want to use it?

For each bucket, record:

1. What I currently have in this bucket
2. Approximate value
3. What I want this bucket to achieve

## 1. Today — Liquidity & Lifestyle

**Definition:** Money designed to be available now or in the near term.

**Typical examples:**

- Cash
- Offset
- Emergency reserve
- Near-term goals

**Question to consider:** What must this money allow me to do over the next 0–3 years?

## 2. Freedom — Pre-Retirement Flexibility

**Definition:** Capital that may help fund choices before super becomes accessible.

**Typical examples:**

- Investments outside super
- Property equity
- Other accessible assets

**Question to consider:** If I wanted work to become optional earlier, what would fund the gap?

|---|---:|---|
|  |  |  |

## 3. Retirement — Long-Term Capital

**Definition:** Assets designed primarily to support life after work.

**Typical examples:**

- Superannuation
- Long-term retirement assets

**Question to consider:** Is this pool on track for the lifestyle I want later?

| What I currently have in this bucket | Approximate value | What I want this bucket to achieve |
|---|---:|---|
|  |  |  |

## The gap to look for

A strong balance sheet can still be poorly aligned to a person’s goals. For example, someone may have significant wealth in their home and super, but limited accessible capital to fund a desired early retirement.

The purpose of the three-bucket view is to expose timing and access gaps, not to prescribe a particular investment.
